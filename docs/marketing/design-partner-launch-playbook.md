# Design Partner Launch Playbook

This playbook turns interested founders into public Singularity mission launches.

## Design Partner Objective

Recruit 5-10 design partners and convert 2-3 of them into public mission launches.

The goal is not to onboard every interested project. The goal is to prove that a qualified project will take costly action:

- Shape a public mission.
- Define a real funding use case.
- Bring its community.
- Co-market the launch.
- Create at least one funding request.

## Design Partner Criteria

Accept a project as a design partner when it has:

- A founder who responds quickly and can make decisions.
- A mission that is clear enough for public storytelling.
- A specific 30-60 day funding need.
- A visible community or audience to activate.
- Public proof of work.
- Willingness to publish about the launch.
- **No live token** and not mid-token launch.
- Willingness to let Singularity handle the crypto layer (white-glove fit).

Do not accept projects that only want liquidity, passive fundraising, equity-only VC intros, or a private demo.

## Design Partner Offer

Use this offer:

> We will help you turn your project into a launchable mission, define the first funding request, and co-market the launch to early supporters.

What Singularity provides:

- Mission positioning.
- Launch page review.
- First funding request framing.
- Launch thread and reply support.
- Investor/supporter feedback loop.
- Weekly inclusion in active mission updates.
- **White-glove crypto setup** for non-crypto founders: wallets, token launch, plain-English copy, supporter onboarding.

What the project must provide:

- Founder time.
- Public launch commitment.
- Mission assets and project links.
- A concrete funding use case.
- Founder launch post.
- Community distribution.
- Post-launch feedback.

## Launch Readiness Checklist

Do not launch until these are true:

- Mission can be explained in one sentence.
- Project has a public proof-of-work link.
- Founder has approved the launch narrative.
- First funding request is specific and credible.
- Supporter call-to-action is clear.
- Founder has a launch post drafted.
- Singularity has a quote post or launch thread drafted.
- Any legal, token, or risk language has been reviewed by the team before public posting.

## White-Glove Launch Checklist (Non-Crypto Partners)

Use this in addition to the standard launch readiness checklist for every non-crypto design partner. All items are blocking gates.

### Founder Setup

- [ ] Founder account created with embedded wallet or social login (Privy).
- [ ] Founder confirms they will not need to explain wallets, bonding curves, or governance in public posts.
- [ ] Singularity team has admin/concierge access to complete launch steps on founder's behalf if needed.

### Copy And Narrative

- [ ] Mission one-liner written in plain English with no crypto jargon.
- [ ] Launch post drafted by Singularity; founder reviews and personalizes.
- [ ] Risk and disclaimer language reviewed by the team before any public posting.
- [ ] FAQ prepared for common founder questions: securities, taxes, "is this crypto?", supporter onboarding.

### Supporter Onboarding

- [ ] Supporter call-to-action tested: can a non-crypto supporter back the mission with minimal friction?
- [ ] Plain-English instructions for first-time backers documented.
- [ ] Fiat or simplified buy flow confirmed if available; fallback path documented if not.

### Launch Mechanics

- [ ] Token launch parameters configured by Singularity team, not founder.
- [ ] Treasury allocation and first funding request framed in mission terms, not tokenomics terms.
- [ ] Founder launch post focuses on mission and use of funds; Singularity handles technical follow-up if asked.

### Post-Launch

- [ ] First-week supporter questions logged and answered in plain English.
- [ ] Founder debrief captures friction points for product UX improvements.
- [ ] Legal/compliance objections from supporters documented for playbook updates.

## Working Session Agenda

Run one 45-minute working session per design partner.

### 1. Mission

Questions:

- What future are you trying to make real?
- Who benefits if the mission works?
- What should supporters be able to understand in 10 seconds?

Output:

> [Project] exists to [plain-English mission].

### 2. Funding Use Case

Questions:

- What work needs funding in the next 30-60 days?
- Who will do the work?
- What result should supporters expect?
- What would be credible evidence that the funds were used well?

Output:

> First funding request: fund [work] so [project] can [outcome].

### 3. Supporter CTA

Questions:

- Why should someone back this now?
- What role can supporters play beyond buying?
- Who should become a council candidate or active participant?

Output:

> Back the mission if you want [future] to exist and want builder funding to happen through a visible onchain process.

### 4. Launch Story

Questions:

- What has already been built?
- Why does this mission need a market and treasury?
- Why is now the right moment?

Output:

> Launch narrative with proof of work, mission, funding request, and supporter CTA.

## Launch Assets

Create these for every launch:

### Mission One-Liner

> [Project] is building [mission] for [audience].

### First Funding Request

> Fund [person/team/workstream] to deliver [output] by [timeframe], helping the mission move from [current state] to [next state].

### Founder Launch Post

> We are launching [project] as a mission on Singularity.
>
> The goal: [mission].
>
> Why now: [proof of work and next milestone].
>
> First funding priority: [specific work].
>
> If you want [future] to exist, back the mission and help decide how builder funding moves.

### Singularity Quote Post

> [Project] is launching a mission to [mission].
>
> The important part: this is not only a token launch. The mission starts with a reserved treasury and a funding path for builders.
>
> Back the mission, follow the first funding request, and help decide what work gets funded.

### Follow-Up Post

> The first funding request for [project] is live: [work].
>
> This is the core Singularity loop: launch a mission, reserve treasury supply, and move builder funding through visible approval.

## Launch Timeline

### T-5 To T-3 Days

- Confirm project fit.
- Run working session.
- Draft mission, funding request, and launch narrative.
- Confirm founder distribution channels.

### T-2 Days

- Finalize launch page assets.
- Draft founder post and Singularity post.
- Prepare warm supporter list.
- Invite 5-10 friendly angels, scouts, or community operators to review.

### Launch Day

- Founder publishes first.
- Singularity quote-posts with the mission framing.
- Founder replies with the first funding request.
- Team shares in relevant communities with context, not spam.
- Track every supporter, reply, question, and objection.

### T+1 To T+7 Days

- Publish the first funding request update.
- Interview the founder about launch friction.
- Ask supporters what made them back or hesitate.
- Ask the founder for two referrals.
- Decide whether the project belongs in future weekly mission digests.

## Design Partner Pipeline

| Stage | Entry Criteria | Exit Criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualified lead | Score 13+ on ICP rubric | Founder reply or warm intro |
| Discovery call | Founder agrees to call | Pain and mission validated |
| Design partner | Founder commits to working session | Launch assets drafted |
| Launch candidate | Mission and funding request are ready | Public launch scheduled |
| Launched mission | Founder publishes | Funding request and supporter activity tracked |
| Reference project | Founder had a useful experience | Founder gives testimonial or referral |

## Go / No-Go Rules

Go when:

- Founder has urgency.
- Funding use case is concrete.
- Mission is easy to explain.
- Founder will co-market.
- There is a plausible first supporter audience.

No-go when:

- The founder will not post publicly.
- The mission sounds like generic token speculation.
- The first funding request is vague.
- There is no audience or proof of work.
- The team wants Singularity to supply all demand.

## First Launch Target

The first launch cycle should produce:

- 5-10 design partner candidates.
- 3 launch-ready projects.
- 2-3 public mission launches.
- 1-3 real funding requests.
- 1 founder referral per launched project.
