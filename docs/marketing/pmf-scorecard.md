# PMF Scorecard

This scorecard measures whether Singularity is solving an urgent enough problem for mission-driven crypto projects.

## Measurement Principle

Do not treat signups, likes, impressions, or Telegram joins as product-market fit.

For Singularity, the best early PMF signal is costly action from both sides of the market:

- Projects publicly launch missions and create funding requests.
- Supporters back missions, return across launches, and participate in funding decisions.

## North Star For The First 30 Days

The first 30-day test succeeds if:

- 10 founder calls happen.
- 5-10 design partners are selected.
- 2-3 missions launch publicly.
- At least 1-3 real funding requests are created.
- At least one founder refers another qualified founder.
- One acquisition segment clearly outperforms the others.

## Project-Side Metrics

Track these for every contacted project:

| Metric | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- |
| Reply rate | Tests whether the problem and message are relevant. |
| Call booking rate | Tests whether founders are curious enough to spend time. |
| Urgent-pain rate | Tests whether the need exists now, not someday. |
| Design-partner conversion | Tests whether founders will commit beyond a call. |
| Public launch conversion | Tests whether trust and positioning are strong enough. |
| Funding request creation | Tests whether the product maps to real work. |
| Founder referral rate | Tests whether the experience is valuable enough to recommend. |

## Supporter-Side Metrics

Track these for each launched mission:

| Metric | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- |
| Unique backing wallets | Measures whether supporters take action. |
| Repeat backers | Shows whether Singularity is becoming a destination, not one-off traffic. |
| Average backing size | Helps distinguish curiosity from conviction. |
| Council candidate registrations | Measures governance intent. |
| Funding request views | Shows whether allocation activity creates interest. |
| Funding request votes | Measures participation beyond buying. |
| Supporter interviews completed | Explains why people backed or hesitated. |

## Qualitative Evidence

Capture exact quotes. PMF often appears in language before it appears in dashboards.

### Strong Signals

- "We were already trying to figure out how to pay contributors."
- "This is better than managing bounties in Discord."
- "I would launch this with my community."
- "Can we use this for our next milestone?"
- "I know another founder who needs this."

### Weak Signals

- "This is interesting."
- "Maybe after our token launch."
- "We mostly just need liquidity."
- "Can you bring investors?"
- "We do not want to post publicly yet."

### Objections To Track

- Legal or compliance concern.
- Token launch timing.
- Concern about looking speculative.
- Lack of community readiness.
- No immediate funded work.
- Investor demand not yet proven.
- Product complexity.

Decision rules:

- Double down on a segment if it produces public launches and funding requests, even if the top-of-funnel is smaller.
- Keep a segment as secondary if it books calls but does not launch.
- Pause a segment if founders are curious but no one has urgent funded work.
- Do not prioritize investors until there are enough credible missions for them to evaluate.

## PMF Thresholds

### Not Yet Working

Signals:

- Founders reply politely but do not book calls.
- Calls produce curiosity, not urgency.
- Founders ask Singularity to bring all investors or demand.
- No one will launch publicly.

Next move:

- Narrow the ICP.
- Rewrite the pitch around a more immediate pain.
- Run 10 more founder discovery calls before building more product.

### Early Pull

Signals:

- Founders book calls from cold or warm outbound.
- At least 30% of calls become design partner candidates.
- Founders can name specific work they need funded.
- Founders agree to co-market.

Next move:

- Focus all distribution on the winning segment.
- Build repeatable launch assets.
- Add lightweight investor/supporter updates.

### Strong Pull

Signals:

- Founders refer other founders.
- Missions attract supporters without only founder hand-holding.
- Funding requests become the reason people revisit.
- Supporters ask for alerts for future missions.
- Investors ask for access to upcoming launches.

Next move:

- Create a recurring launch calendar.
- Publish weekly mission digests.
- Start investor-side onboarding and watchlists.
- Build tooling for repeat launches and supporter retention.

## Weekly Operating Cadence

Every Friday:

1. Update the weekly scorecard.
2. Pick the strongest five founder quotes.
3. Review the segment decision matrix.
4. Decide which message gets the next batch of leads.
5. Choose the next launch candidate or pause weak candidates.
6. Ask every launched founder for referrals.

The campaign should only scale after a segment creates repeatable launches, not just conversations.
