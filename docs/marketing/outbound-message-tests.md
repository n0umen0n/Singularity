# Outbound Message Tests

This document defines the first outbound campaign for testing Singularity's product-market fit with project founders.

## Campaign Goal

Book founder calls, not signups.

The first test should learn whether non-crypto founders have urgent fundraising pain, are willing to launch publicly, and will trust Singularity to handle the token and crypto layer.

## Primary ICP Reminder

Best fit: **mission-driven projects outside crypto** with visible proof of work, a real community, and difficulty raising through traditional channels — Singularity handles the token and launch mechanics.

Wrong fit: already has a token, crypto-native looking for liquidity, equity-only fundraising with no public launch willingness, or unwilling to co-market.

## Test Structure

Run four message angles in parallel:

1. **Stuck fundraising** (primary — use alone during discovery sprint).
2. Contributor funding.
3. Community-aligned raise.
4. Mission launch.

Send each angle to a similar quality batch of leads. Do not judge message performance from mixed-quality audiences.

Recommended first batch (discovery sprint uses angle 1 only):

| Angle | Leads | Target |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Stuck fundraising | 15 | Founder calls |
| Contributor funding | 15 | Founder calls |
| Community-aligned raise | 15 | Founder calls |
| Mission launch | 15 | Founder calls |

Winning angle is the one that creates the highest number of qualified replies and booked calls, not the highest likes or impressions.

## Message Principles

Every message should:

- Reference something specific the project is building.
- Name one likely funding use case.
- Avoid sounding like a generic token launch pitch.
- Avoid requiring crypto literacy from the founder.
- Ask for a conversation, not a signup.
- Be short enough to work as a DM or reply.
- Make Singularity sound like a white-glove launch partner, not self-serve crypto infra.

Avoid:

- "Revolutionary", "disrupt", "democratize", and vague crypto language.
- Overpromising fundraising outcomes.
- Legal ownership language.
- Suggesting passive guaranteed returns.
- Sending the same message to every project.
- Assuming the founder knows wallets, tokens, or onchain mechanics.

## Angle 1: Stuck Fundraising (Primary)

Use when a project shows signs of fundraising difficulty: slow raise, grant rejection, bootstrapped past runway, or public posts about capital needs. **Use this angle alone during the discovery sprint.**

### DM

> Saw what you're building with [project]. Looks like real progress — curious if traditional fundraising has been slower or harder than expected?
>
> Singularity helps mission-driven projects launch a public funding market and treasury. We handle the crypto layer; you focus on the mission and launch story.
>
> Worth a 15 min founder call this week? Looking for a few early design partners.

### Short Reply

> If raising through traditional channels has been the bottleneck, Singularity is built for that: turn the mission into a public market with a treasury — without you becoming a crypto company.

### Follow-Up

> Quick follow-up. The design partner offer is hands-on: we shape the mission, first funding use case, and launch story, and run the token mechanics. If fundraising isn't the priority right now, no worries.

## Angle 2: Contributor Funding

Use when a project mentions contributors, hiring, audits, content, growth, integrations, or dev help — even if the primary pain is operational rather than fundraising.

### DM

> Saw what you're building with [project]. Are you currently trying to fund contributors, contractors, or early team work?
>
> Singularity helps projects launch a mission market with treasury funding built in, so work can be funded through a visible approval flow. We handle the crypto setup.
>
> Worth a 15 min founder call this week? I am looking for a few design partners we can help launch and promote.

### Short Reply

> If the next step is funding contributors or early work, Singularity is built for that: launch a mission market, reserve treasury supply, and fund builders — we run the crypto layer.

### Follow-Up

> Quick follow-up. The design partner offer is hands-on: we help shape the mission, first funding request, and launch story. If contributor funding is not a priority right now, no worries.

## Angle 3: Community-Aligned Raise

Use when a project has a community, waitlist, newsletter, or strong public audience.

### DM

> [Project] already has the kind of mission people can rally around.
>
> One thing we are testing with Singularity: instead of raising only from insiders, a project can launch a mission market where the community backs the mission and treasury funding is visible from day one. We handle the crypto mechanics.
>
> Would you be open to a 15 min call? We are choosing a small group of first launch partners.

### Short Reply

> This feels like the kind of mission that could work well as a community-backed market — especially if your audience wants to back the work, not just watch from the sidelines.

### Follow-Up

> The useful test is whether your community would back a concrete mission and see how funding moves to the work that matters. Happy to walk through the flow if useful.

## Angle 4: Mission Launch

Use when the project has a crisp public mission but the immediate funding pain is not obvious.

### DM

> Your mission for [project] is clear: [mission in plain English].
>
> Singularity turns missions like that into public markets with a treasury and funding path for the work. We are looking for a few early projects to launch with hands-on positioning and distribution support — we handle the crypto layer.
>
> Open to a short call this week?

### Short Reply

> This is a clear mission. Singularity is built for turning missions like this into public funding markets with a treasury for the work built in.

### Follow-Up

> I think the first useful step would be mapping one concrete funding use case that would move [project] forward. If that exists, the mission is probably a strong fit.

## Warm Intro Request

Use this with investors, scouts, operators, and founder friends.

> I am looking for 5-10 design partners for Singularity.
>
> Best fit: mission-driven projects outside crypto with visible proof of work, a real community, and difficulty raising through traditional channels — Singularity handles the token and launch mechanics.
>
> Who is building something real and would benefit from a public community-backed funding launch?

## Founder Call Script

Keep calls focused on pain and commitment.

### Opening

> I am not trying to sell a finished product today. I am testing whether Singularity solves a real fundraising and mission-funding problem for projects like yours — without you needing to become a crypto expert.

### Discovery Questions

1. What is the mission in your own words?
2. What work most needs to happen in the next 30-60 days?
3. Who would do that work?
4. Have you tried raising through angels, VCs, or grants? What happened?
5. How would you fund that work today?
6. Where does the current process break down?
7. Would you launch publicly if Singularity handled wallets, tokens, and the crypto layer?
8. What would make you uncomfortable about a community-backed funding launch?
9. If we helped with positioning and distribution, would you commit to a first mission launch?

### Close

If strong fit:

> I think there is enough here to design a launch around one concrete funding use case. Next step would be a 45 min working session where we draft the mission, funding use case, and launch story.

If weak fit:

> This may be too early for a mission launch, but I would still value a referral to one founder who is actively struggling to raise and has visible proof of work.

## Response Labels

Tag every reply:

- `urgent-pain`: Founder has a live fundraising or capital bottleneck.
- `curious`: Founder likes the idea but has no immediate use case.
- `investor-first`: Founder mostly wants VC intros or equity-only capital.
- `equity-only`: Founder will not consider community-backed token mechanics.
- `too-early`: No proof of work or community yet.
- `wrong-fit`: Already has a token, crypto-native looking for liquidity, or not mission-based.
- `design-partner`: Founder is willing to co-design a launch under white-glove terms.

## Decision Rules

After the first 60 contacts:

- If stuck fundraising books the most calls, lead with non-crypto capital-formation messaging and narrow ICP around fundraising-stuck missions.
- If contributor funding wins, focus on teams with immediate funded work (may overlap with OSS and indie SaaS).
- If community-aligned raise wins, focus on projects with active non-crypto audiences.
- If mission launch wins, improve landing and launch storytelling before adding investor features.
- If none wins, the audience is wrong or the pitch is too abstract. Re-interview warm founders before sending more outbound.

Do not scale automation until one message creates repeatable founder conversations.
