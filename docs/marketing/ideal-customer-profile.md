# Singularity Ideal Customer Profile

This document defines the first customer segment for Singularity's product-market fit test.

## Primary ICP

The first users should be **high-quality, mission-driven projects outside crypto** with **no existing token**, struggling to raise through traditional channels, and willing to co-market a public launch.

Singularity handles the token layer, wallets, launch mechanics, and supporter onboarding. Founders are not expected to understand bonding curves, governance mechanics, or crypto infrastructure.

Singularity should not begin by selling to passive investors. Investors need credible missions, public launches, and proof that projects can attract builders and supporters. Projects create that supply.

## Best-Fit Project

A best-fit first project has these traits:

- The founder or team is already building in public on X or another reachable channel.
- The project has a concrete mission that can be understood in one sentence by a non-crypto audience.
- There is strong proof of work: shipped product, demo, GitHub traction, waitlist, research output, or active community.
- The team has a real 30–60 day capital need: hiring, infra, research, content, partnerships, or launch support — not vague "we need money."
- The founder is willing to co-market and explain the mission publicly.
- The project has **no live token** and is not mid-token launch.
- **Token readiness = willingness, not literacy** — the founder trusts Singularity to run the crypto layer.
- The project is early enough to need distribution, but mature enough that supporters can evaluate it.

## Strongest Segments (Primary)

### 1. VC-Rejected But Credible Startups

Projects with strong demo or traction, clear mission, and a cap table that has not closed through traditional channels.

Common signals:

- Building in public with regular progress updates.
- Public posts about fundraising struggles, slow raises, or running out of runway.
- Strong product but passed over by angels or seed funds.
- Clear next milestone blocked by capital.

Primary message:

> You have a mission people believe in but traditional fundraising isn't working. Singularity turns that mission into a public market and treasury — we handle the crypto layer; you focus on the mission and launch story.

### 2. Open-Source And Public-Goods Projects

Projects with proven usage but no clean monetization path or sustainable grant funding.

Common signals:

- Active GitHub repo with real stars, forks, or dependents.
- Maintainer posting about sustainability, sponsorship gaps, or burnout.
- Public goods with visible impact but weak revenue model.
- Community of users who would back the project if given a path.

Primary message:

> Your project already has users who believe in the mission. Singularity gives them a way to back it publicly — with a treasury and funding flow built in from day one.

### 3. Mission-First Indie Products

Climate, education, health, creative tools, local infrastructure, and other mission-driven products underserved by traditional angels.

Common signals:

- Product Hunt launch, newsletter audience, or niche community.
- Founder talks about impact, users, or mission — not token timing.
- Bootstrapped past comfortable runway.
- Clear use of funds for the next product milestone.

Primary message:

> Strong missions stall when capital only flows to hype or insider networks. Singularity turns yours into a public funding market — without you becoming a crypto company.

### 4. Research And Builder Collectives

Teams or collectives with visible output but difficulty converting credibility into capital.

Common signals:

- Published research, open datasets, or public tooling.
- Academic or institutional adjacency without institutional funding secured.
- Active collaborators who need to be paid for the next phase.
- Founder willing to launch publicly with Singularity's support.

Primary message:

> You already have proof the work matters. Singularity helps turn that credibility into a mission market with a treasury for the next phase of work.

## Secondary ICP

Use as a secondary segment after the non-crypto primary test validates. Do not lead outbound with this segment.

**Pre-token crypto-adjacent projects** — hackathon alumni, grant recipients, and builders without a launched token. Same logic applies: Singularity creates the first token layer. Lower priority because ecosystem noise, launchpad competition, and holder attention dilution remain high.

Common signals:

- Recent hackathon finalist or grant recipient without a token launch.
- Public roadmap with unfunded next milestones.
- Active X or Discord community but no live token.

Primary message:

> You already have a mission people can understand. Singularity can help turn that mission into a market with builder funding built in — as your first token layer.

## Exclusions

Do not prioritize these projects in the first PMF cycle:

- Projects with a **live token** or active token launch in progress.
- Idea-stage projects with no public proof of work.
- Anonymous accounts with no trust surface.
- Teams only asking for passive liquidity, token hype, or exchange attention.
- Mature projects that already have venture funding, launch infrastructure, and contributor programs.
- Projects whose mission is too vague to explain without a long pitch.
- Projects that cannot or will not co-market.
- Teams that need equity-only institutional capital and will never accept community-backed token mechanics.

## Qualification Questions

Use these questions in founder calls:

1. What work would move the mission forward in the next 30-60 days?
2. Who should be paid to do that work?
3. Have you tried raising through angels, VCs, or grants? What happened?
4. How are you funding that work today?
5. Where does the current process feel slow, opaque, or blocked?
6. Would you publicly launch a mission page if Singularity handled the token and crypto layer?
7. What would make you uncomfortable about a community-backed funding launch?
8. Who are two other founders with the same problem?

## Scoring Rubric

Score every lead from 0-3 on each dimension.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mission clarity | Vague idea | Understandable after explanation | Clear one-line mission | Memorable and public-facing |
| Proof of work | None | Early concept | Demo, repo, or community | Shipped product or strong traction |
| Funding pain | None | Future concern | Active capital need | Urgent funded-work bottleneck |
| Founder energy | Passive | Interested | Responsive and thoughtful | Willing to co-market quickly |
| Community surface | None | Small audience | Active X, Discord, or newsletter | Audience likely to take action |
| Token readiness | Refuses token concept | Curious but hesitant | Willing with guidance | Eager; trusts Singularity to run it |

Prioritize leads scoring 13 or higher. Only take calls below that threshold if the founder has a direct relationship or unusual strategic value.

**Hard disqualifiers (automatic exclude regardless of score):**

- Live token or mid-token launch
- Primary goal is liquidity or exchange listing
- Unwilling to launch publicly

## First-Cycle Goal

The first ICP cycle succeeds if:

- 20 qualified non-crypto projects are identified (discovery sprint).
- 15 founders are contacted.
- 5 founder calls are booked and completed.
- ≥2 founders would launch publicly if Singularity runs token mechanics.
- 5-10 design partners are selected from validated segments.
- 2-3 projects publicly launch missions.
- At least one launched founder refers another qualified founder.

See [`discovery-sprint-non-crypto.md`](discovery-sprint-non-crypto.md) for the 2-week validation sprint workflow.
